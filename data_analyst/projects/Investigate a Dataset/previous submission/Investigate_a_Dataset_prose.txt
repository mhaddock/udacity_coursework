Introduction

We will be analyzing a dataset containing information about medical appointments made between Nov 10, 2015 and June 8, 2016 in the city of Vitória, Brazil. The data was sourced from Kaggle: Medical Appointment No-Shows. It contains data on 110K+ medical appointments with information about each appointment and the patient.

The aim of this analysis is to identify data points that show statistically significant associations with appointment no-shows that are large enough to investigate further. This is intended as a screening pass over the data to help focus future investigations. The results here will not include any predictive conclusions or suggestions for modifying certain factors to achieve a desired result.

[cell 40]
Patient-level Analysis Considerations
The dataset is organized as one row per appointment. To investigate patient-level data points, we will need to take care we don't overcount patients with multiple appointments.

We have previously verified that each patient's demographic and health data points remain constant across all their appointments; _see Data Cleaning/PatientId_. However, whether a patient attends or misses a given appointment can vary, and the number of appointments scheduled per patient also varies. 

To prevent patients with multiple appointments skewing the analysis, we will treat each patient as a single data row. To represent their show/no-show behavior, we will use the proportion of missed to scheduled appointments.

$$\text{p\_NoShow} = \frac{\text{num\_patient\_NoShow}}{\text{num\_patient\_appointments}}$$

For simplicity we will also group patients into "likely no-show" or "likely show" buckets:

| classification | threshold |

| likely no-show | p_NoShow >= 0.5 |
| likely show | p_NoShow < 0.5 |

[cell 42]

Both threshold values are based on "rule of thumb" conventions. See Caveats above.

Goals and Acceptance Criteria
The primary aim of this analysis was to identify data points that have a statistically significant association with appointment no-shows, and that the association was large enough to be meaningful.

| criteria | Threshold |

| Statistical Significance | p-value  0.01 |
| Effect size (appointment level data) | pseudo R^2 > 0.01 |

Results
Patient: Health and Demographic Information
While many of the data points demonstrated a statistically significant association, none cleared our threshold for effect size.

Appointment: Logistical Information
Similarly, most of the data points demonstrated a statistically significant association, but only lead time (num_days) also met our criteria for effect size. 

Caveats
I have limited my selection of regression models and "rule of thumb" conventions for interpreting results to ones I have experience with in my coursework. Other models or interpretations may exist that would produce superior results.

In some cases, sparsity of data may have negative effects on the quality of the regression models, specifically Neighborhood and NumHandicaps.

In patient level tests, all patients carry equal weight although some may have many more appointments than others. This could be obscuring differences in behavior that might track with the number of appointments a patient has made.

Tests on SMS_received intentionally ignored 38,562 same-day appointments out of the 110,521 total appointments. The dataset for SMS_received tests is significantly smaller than used for the tests on the other logistical appointment data points. 

Synergistic relationships between health, demographic, or logistic factors may exist, but were unexplored here with the exception of a cursory investigation of possible relationships between num_days and SMS_received. Evidence to support further investigation was not found in that case.
